About the model

Catchment Area Neural Network Model

A small fully connected network that estimates subcatchment runoff from the same properties SWMM uses, so it can be checked against a full SWMM run.

{# calculations.js draws the diagram from #calculations-network. #}

Inside the network

The eight inputs are standardised with the mean and spread of the training data, then pass through two hidden layers of eight neurons to one output neuron. Each neuron adds up its weighted inputs and a bias and applies ReLU: a negative sum switches it off. The network was trained with the Adam optimiser on mean squared error.

The equations behind the network

Features

The network reads eight properties of each subcatchment from the SWMM model, in SI units (a US model's widths and storage depths are converted first):

Imperviousness (%)
Share of the land surface, excluding LID, that is impervious.
Width (m)
Characteristic width of the overland flow path.
Slope (%)
Average surface slope.
Manning's n, impervious
Roughness for overland flow over the impervious area.
Manning's n, pervious
Roughness for overland flow over the pervious area.
Depression storage, impervious (mm)
Depth of water held back on the impervious area.
Depression storage, pervious (mm)
Depth of water held back on the pervious area.
Impervious area without storage (%)
Share of the impervious area without depression storage.

The area is not among them: the network learned from subcatchments of 5 ha, so its prediction does not change with the area.

Applications
  • Water resource management: predicting catchment runoff informs water allocation and conservation decisions.
  • Flood control: understanding what drives runoff helps locate areas at risk and design mitigation measures.
  • Environmental conservation: estimating the effect of land-use or climate changes supports conservation strategies.
